Community kitchen

A self-managed project that transforms food and people at the heart of the neighbourhood. We rescue what local shops can no longer sell and turn it into meals, cooking together.

Call us Email us

How it began

The idea came up during the pandemic, out of concern several people shared about growing food waste and the urgent need to feed people without a home, at the same time as strengthening neighbourhood ties in an urban setting that is often too individualised.

Since then, the kitchen has become a meeting point where people share experience, know-how and, above all, recipes.

How it works

The routine is simple but effective:

  • On Mondays or Tuesdays we collect food from local shops and markets that can no longer be sold because of appearance, a best-before date coming up, or minor blemishes, but which is still perfectly good to eat.
  • We sort it by condition and, depending on what we have, put together recipes.
  • On Tuesdays we cook together at the Menjador Ca la Rosa premises.
  • Participants bring ideas, adapt to whatever food has come in, and share out the prep and cooking.
  • At the end, everyone takes home a container of what's been cooked.

Building community through cooking

The group is made up of 8 to 14 people from very different backgrounds, each with their own culinary tradition. That turns every session into a celebration of flavours and stories, and a chance to discover ingredients, techniques and ways of cooking that break down assumptions.

Cooking together is also a way to talk and support one another. The people who take part share everyday concerns, ideas and challenges, and build a support network that often reaches well beyond the kitchen.

Why it's worth rescuing food

Cutting food waste

Rescuing food that shops can't sell gives a second life to produce that, though still nutritious, would otherwise end up in the bin. It directly cuts waste and helps the neighbourhood see the real value of food.

Saving money

Rescued food means full meals cooked at very low cost, which particularly helps families or people with tight budgets. Sharing know-how about storing and cooking it helps everyone get more out of it at home too.

Healthy eating

Food headed for the bin is often fruit and vegetables that, while not "perfect" looking, are perfectly healthy and nutritious. At the community kitchen, people learn to give it the value it deserves.

Cultural exchange

Everyone brings a recipe, a technique or a story. The shared recipe book pulls in dishes from all over the world, adapted to whatever food is available: cooking that is creative, inclusive and full of surprises, where everyone can learn and teach.

Impact on the neighbourhood

On one hand, the community kitchen helps cut food waste and encourages more responsible eating. On the other, it builds social cohesion, inclusion and confidence for people who often don't have a space to connect with others nearby.

The project also opens up debate about how we produce and consume food today, and makes the case for the right to decent, sustainable food for everyone.

Challenges and what's next

Like any community project, it faces real challenges: keeping food donations coming, finding the right spaces, keeping volunteers motivated and reaching more people in the neighbourhood. The response so far has been very positive, and the project already runs on its own steam.

Looking ahead, the plan is to build the kitchen into a real reference point for sustainability and social cohesion, and to widen what it offers: healthy cooking workshops, talks on food waste, and activities for families.

Questions about the community kitchen

How can I join in?

Write to us and we'll tell you when the next session is. You don't need to know how to cook or have any experience: everyone brings what they know.

I run a shop in the neighbourhood and would like to donate food. How do I do that?

Write to cuinacomunitarialasagrera@menjadorcalarosa.com and we'll sort it out. We collect on Mondays or Tuesdays.

What happens to what gets cooked?

At the end of the session, participants take home a container of what's been cooked.

Does it cost anything to take part?

It's a self-managed, community project. Write to us and we'll talk it through.

Tell us what you need

Call or email us. We reply with a real proposal and a fixed price, with no obligation and no endless forms.
